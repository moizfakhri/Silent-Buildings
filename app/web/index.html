    Methodology, Data Scope & Honest Limits

    Independent civic research — not a legal finding about any owner or tenant. Per-building counts come directly from NYC Open Data (HPD Violations, HPD Complaints, 311, OMO Emergency Repair Charges, Litigations, Vacate Orders, AEP, Evictions, DOB Violations, DOF Sales, PLUTO, Building Footprints) covering 101,442 Bronx and Brooklyn residential lots with 3+ units (2018–2025).

    • Privacy by construction: Zero owner, landlord, managing agent, respondent, or tenant names are stored or displayed anywhere. Portfolio links use anonymized hashes of normalized HPD business addresses.
    • Original "Silent Distress" result reported honestly: On its own, a building’s lack of complaints does not predict hidden extra danger (observed/predicted 0.84; matched RR 0.65). However, portfolio context changes the picture: among completely quiet buildings (0 complaints & 0 violations), sister-building Class C distress strongly predicts next-year Class C hazards (OR 1.35, p < 10-34) and serious events (OR 1.31).
    • Caveats: HPD registration contacts reflect the current snapshot (mitigated via a never-sold stability check); model probabilities are wrong for many individual buildings; enforcement records reflect reporting and inspection capacity as well as physical condition.
